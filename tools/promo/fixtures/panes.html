Panes, the tmux way

A pane is a page that shares the window. Split once and the docs sit next to the code review; split again and the dashboard joins them. Each pane keeps its own mode, its own scroll position and its own history. The layout cheat sheet lists every split on one page.

The prefix does the work: ctrl+b v splits right, ctrl+b - splits down, ctrl+b h j k l moves between panes, and ctrl+b z zooms the current pane to the full window and back.

Why panes beat windows

Windows overlap, panes never do. A pane layout is a tree, so it survives a restart, and a workspace keeps its own layout while you are away in another.

Close a pane and its tab stays; zoom a pane and the others wait, exactly where you left them.

Layouts that build themselves

tmux names its layouts, and so does the browser: even rows, even columns, a main pane with the rest stacked beside it, and a tiled grid. One key cycles through them, so the pane you read stays where your eyes are while the others make room.

A dwindle spiral splits the longer side each time. Four pages fit a laptop screen without a single drag, and a fifth halves the smallest pane rather than the one you are reading.

Moving between panes

The same four keys that move the cursor in the editor move the focus between panes. The focused pane wears the theme's accent on its border, so you always know which page the next key goes to.

Resizing is a mode of its own: enter it, tap a direction until the split feels right, and leave it again. Nothing to aim at, no border to grab.

When a pane is done

Closing a pane never closes its tab. The page goes back to the tab list, scrolled where you left it, and the remaining panes grow to fill the space.

Break a pane out into a tab of its own, or bring a tab back in as a pane: the layout is a tree, and a tree is easy to rearrange from the keyboard.
